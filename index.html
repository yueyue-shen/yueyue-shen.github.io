01 / About

A little
about me.

Bologna, Italy
Beijing, China

I am a Ph.D. student in astrophysics at the National Astronomical Observatories of China (NAOC), Chinese Academy of Sciences, supervised by Prof. Chao Liu. From September 2025 to September 2026, I was a visiting student at INAF-OAS Bologna, working with Dr. Angela Bragaglia and Dr. Emanuele Dalessandro.

My research focuses on young star clusters in the Milky Way. I combine Gaia astrometry and photometry, LAMOST spectroscopy, N-body simulations, and Bayesian modelling to study cluster membership, stellar mass functions, cluster chemical and kinematic properties, and multiple-cluster systems.

My Ph.D. project, The Stellar Initial Mass Function and Early Evolution of Young Stellar Systems, seeks to connect the present-day properties of young clusters with their initial conditions. A central question is how to distinguish the imprint of the initial mass function from the effects of stellar evolution, cluster dynamics, and observational selection. Through this work, I aim to understand what young clusters can reveal about the formation and early evolution of stellar populations.

Beyond individual clusters, I investigate how young stellar systems are connected through their chemical compositions, motions, and spatial structure. This research explores hierarchical star formation and the role of the Galactic environment in shaping the evolution of clusters and multiple-cluster systems. My earlier work applied machine learning to infer stellar distances and parameters from spectroscopic surveys, providing a complementary perspective on the study of stellar populations across the Milky Way.

02 / Research

Young stellar systems: origins and evolution.

Connecting stellar initial conditions with the present-day properties of young clusters through N-body simulations, Bayesian colour–magnitude diagram modelling, Gaia astrometry, and LAMOST spectroscopy.

I

IMF-aware membership selection

I use N-body forward modelling and synthetic Gaia observations to investigate how the assumed stellar initial mass function and cluster evolution affect membership identification. This framework quantifies completeness and contamination, helping assess how selection effects shape the stellar populations recovered from observations.

Gaia · PeTar · Forward modelling

II

Cluster properties from colour–magnitude diagrams

With STARCAT, the Python package I developed, I fit synthetic colour–magnitude diagrams to observations to infer the properties of young star clusters. This Bayesian approach jointly models present-day mass function slopes, age, extinction, and unresolved binary properties while accounting for membership selection effects.

STARCAT · Bayesian inference · Colour–magnitude diagrams

III

Cluster chemistry & kinematics

Combining LAMOST spectroscopy with Gaia astrometry, I build a homogeneous catalogue of metallicities and radial velocities for young Galactic clusters. These measurements support cluster age estimates and studies of young-disc kinematics.

LAMOST · Metallicities · Radial velocities

IV

Multiple-cluster systems

Searching for LISCA-like systems through spatial structure and phase-space information to study hierarchical assembly, coeval formation, and Galactic environmental effects.

Gaia · Cluster structure · Kinematics

Background on LISCA systems:
LISCA I — Dalessandro et al. (2021)
LISCA II — Della Croce et al. (2023)
